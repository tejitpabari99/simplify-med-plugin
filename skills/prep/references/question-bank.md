# Question Bank

Use this bank to choose topics and phrasing for the patient's questions. Pick only what fits the patient's concerns, goal, visit type, or supplied instructions. Adapt wording to the patient's situation and words. Never present every question.

Sources: the "Questions generation" layered proposal (universal questions plus expandable topic questions) and the AHRQ Question Builder lists it records.

## Ranking

1. The patient's top concern, goal, or unresolved question.
2. Missing, unclear, or conflicting appointment instructions ("Confirm with the clinic").
3. A decision the patient said they face (options, benefits and risks, waiting).
4. Universal questions, only to fill a sparse list and never duplicating a specific one.

Default: 3–5 primary questions. Put other relevant questions under "If there is time".

## Rules

- One issue per question. Plain words. Phrase it to ask the clinician.
- Do not embed an assumption: ask "What could be causing my headaches?", not "Is my headache caused by my new medication?", unless the patient themself raised that link, in which case keep it as their question.
- Do not name a condition, test, or medication the patient or supplied material did not mention.
- Questions about warning signs ask the clinician what to watch for; never list warning signs yourself.

## Layer 1: Universal questions

1. What is the main thing I should understand from today's visit?
2. What should we do next, and how soon?
3. What should I do at home before the next visit?
4. What signs would mean things are improving or getting worse?
5. When should we follow up, and what will we check then?

## Layer 2: Topic questions

### Understanding the concern or result
- What is the main takeaway?
- How serious is this?
- Is this expected to improve, stay the same, or get worse?
- Is this certain, or do we need more information?
- What might be causing this?
- Could something else explain these symptoms?

### Care plan
- What are the next steps?
- What should I do at home, and how often?
- How long should I continue?
- What is the most important part of the plan?

### Treatment options
- What are my options?
- Which option do you recommend and why?
- What are the benefits and risks?
- How will we know if it is working?
- What happens if we wait?
- How can I find out what it might cost?

### Monitoring
- What should I track at home?
- When should I call your office?
- When is this urgent?

### Follow-up
- When should I come back?
- What should improve by then?
- What happens if things do not improve?

### Daily life
- Can I continue my normal activities?
- Should I avoid anything?
- Could this affect school, work, sleep, exercise, eating, mood, or independence?
- Are there supports or accommodations that could help?

### Medication
- What is this medication for?
- How and when should I take it, and for how long?
- What side effects should I watch for?
- What should I do if I miss a dose?
- Are there interactions with food, alcohol, supplements, or other medications?
- How will we decide whether to continue, stop, or adjust it?

### Tests
- What is the test for, and how is it done?
- What do I need to do to prepare for it?
- When will I get the results, and who will give them to me?
- What is the next step after the test?

### Surgery or procedure
- Why do I need it, and what happens if I wait?
- How long will it take, and will I need anesthesia?
- How long will recovery take?
- Will I need help getting home or at home afterward?

### Referral
- Why am I being referred?
- How soon should I schedule it?
- What should I bring or prepare?
