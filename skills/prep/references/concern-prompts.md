# Concern Prompts

Optional follow-ups for each concern the patient raises. Ask only the ones that fit the concern. Every prompt accepts "not sure", "skip", or free text. Never require an answer and never interpret the answer medically.

Source: the "Prep the patient for his appointment" proposal (patients answer only relevant questions; the result is a concise summary to share with the doctor), with AHRQ Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit guidance to use the patient's words, prioritize key points, and bring a written question list (Tools #4 and #14).

## Opening prompts

- **Concerns:** What symptoms or concerns would you like to discuss? Describe them in your own words. (Then ask them to put them in priority order.)
- **Goal:** What do you hope to get from this appointment?

## Per-concern follow-ups

| Topic | Prompt | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Where in your body do you notice it? For example chest, stomach, head, back, right knee, left eye. | Skip for non-body concerns. |
| Onset | When did you first notice it? Did it come on suddenly or gradually? | Keep approximate timing approximate. |
| Possible triggers | Do you think something may have caused or contributed to it? For example an injury, illness, medication, stress, a new activity, a fall, a change in routine, food, or sleep. | Record the patient's belief as theirs. Never confirm or suggest a cause. |
| Duration | When it happens, does it last seconds, minutes, hours, or all day? | |
| Frequency | Does it happen once in a while, every day, several times a day, or only during certain activities? | |
| Severity | How severe is it? For pain, 0 is no pain and 10 is the worst pain you can imagine. | Offer only when useful. Never translate a rating into urgency. |
| Daily impact | Is it affecting your sleep, appetite, mood, work, school, movement, memory, daily activities, or independence? | |
| Better or worse | Does anything make it better or worse? For example rest, movement, food, stress, medication, time of day, position, or activity. | |
| Already tried | Have you tried any medication, home treatment, lifestyle change, or other strategy? Did it help? | Record exactly what they report; do not evaluate it. |

For a follow-up visit, it can help to ask: What has changed since your last visit? For a test or procedure, ask whether they received preparation instructions and what they said.

## Optional things-to-bring suggestions

Offer these as questions the patient may accept or decline. They are organizing suggestions, never clinic requirements.

- A written list of your questions (this brief).
- A current list of medications, vitamins, and supplements, if you keep one.
- Recent home readings or a symptom log, if you have been keeping one.
- Recent records or results from other clinicians, if relevant to this visit.
- Insurance card and photo ID, unless the clinic's instructions already cover them.
- A family member or friend to help listen and take notes, if you would like.

## Wording guidance

- Use the patient's words for their concerns. Shorten only with their approval.
- One idea per sentence. Common words. Most important item first.
- Never ask for a full medical history or every medication by default.
