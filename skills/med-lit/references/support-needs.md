# Support Needs and Preferences

Optional questions asked after the screen, or on their own in the support-needs-only path. All are skippable. Record answers in the patient's words. These are preferences, never measurements, and never change or imply a score.

Basis: Healthy People 2030 defines personal health literacy as the ability to find, understand, and use information and services for health decisions, and notes it is contextual. The AHRQ Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit recommends clear communication for everyone, attention to language preferences, and inviting questions.

## Questions

1. **Language:** Which language do you prefer for health information? For talking with your care team?
2. **Format:** How do you prefer to get health information: written, spoken, pictures or diagrams, video, or a mix?
3. **Hardest tasks:** Which of these, if any, are hard for you? Filling out forms; reading instructions or letters; understanding test results or numbers; knowing which questions to ask; using the patient portal or health apps; keeping track of medications; comparing treatment options. (Let them pick any, none, or add their own.)
4. **Helpful supports:** What would help most? For example plain-language summaries, a written list of questions before visits, bringing a family member or friend, an interpreter, larger print, having the clinician explain back or check understanding, more time to ask questions.
5. **Helper:** Is there someone who usually helps you with health information? (Record only the relationship, not a name, unless the patient wants it included.)
6. **Anything else:** Is there anything else that makes health information easier or harder for you?

## Recording rules

- Map task answers to `observed_task_needs` only when the patient reported them; label each `self_reported`.
- Do not add needs the patient did not state.
- Do not treat a language preference, disability accommodation, or support request as evidence of limited literacy.
